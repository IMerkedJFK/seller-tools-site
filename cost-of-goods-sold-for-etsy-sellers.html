Cost of goods sold for Etsy sellers

Cost of goods sold, often shortened to COGS, is what it cost you to make or buy the products you actually sold. It matters because it comes off your sales before you get to profit, and for US sellers it also reduces the net profit your taxes are based on. Reviewed November 2026. Confirm the details at irs.gov.

What usually counts

For a maker, COGS generally includes the materials that end up in the product, such as wax, wicks, jars and labels for a candle. Buying wholesale goods to resell counts too. The key idea is that you deduct the cost of items sold, not everything you bought. Unsold stock sits in inventory until it sells.

What usually stays out

Etsy listing, transaction and processing fees, ad spend, shipping labels and software are normally tracked as separate business expenses, not product cost. How to treat each item can depend on your situation, so check the IRS guidance or ask a tax professional.

Per unit worked example

Say one batch makes 50 candles. Wax is $60, wicks $15, jars $75 and labels $10. The batch costs 60 + 15 + 75 + 10 = $160. Divide by 50 and each candle costs $3.20. If you sell 30 candles from the batch, the cost of those sales is 30 x $3.20 = $96. The other 20 candles ($64) remain in inventory.

Year level example

A common formula is beginning inventory plus purchases, minus ending inventory. Suppose you started the year with $300 of supplies and stock, bought $1,200 more, and counted $250 left at year end. COGS is 300 + 1,200 minus 250 = $1,250. If sales were $8,000, gross profit is 8,000 minus 1,250 = $6,750, before fees, shipping and other expenses.

Why per unit cost helps pricing

If you do not know your real cost per item, you cannot know your margin. Enter your unit cost into the free Etsy fee calculator to see what is left after fees. For the pricing side, see the break even price formula.

Keep the records

Save supplier receipts, note batch sizes, and do a count of inventory at year end. The Sales and Expense Tracker gives you a simple place to log purchases and sales together. Learn how profit becomes taxable income in the self employment tax guide.
