How to price handmade products on Etsy

Many new sellers copy what similar shops charge. That only works if their costs match yours. A better way is to start from your own numbers.

Step 1: add up your costs for one item

Materials, packaging, the shipping label if you pay for it, and a fair amount for your time. Put a real hourly wage on your work, even a modest one, so your time is not free.

Step 2: choose a profit margin

Pick the share of the sale price you want to keep after costs and fees. Forty percent is a common starting point for handmade goods.

Step 3: account for Etsy fees

Fees are $0.20 listing, 6.5 percent transaction, and 3 percent plus $0.25 payment processing, which together are about 9.5 percent plus $0.45 per sale.

Step 4: use the formula

This formula assumes US fees, no Offsite Ads, no currency conversion, and ignores the small extra processing fee on any sales tax. Buyer total equals (0.45 plus your costs) divided by (0.905 minus your margin as a decimal). With $4 of costs and a 40 percent margin, the buyer total is 4.45 divided by 0.505, which is $8.81. Subtract any shipping you charge to get the item price.

Then compare the result to the market. If it is higher than what buyers will pay, look for ways to cut costs or add value, not for ways to skip your own margin.
